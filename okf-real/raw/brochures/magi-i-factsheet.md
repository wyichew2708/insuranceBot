FUND MANAGER'S COMMENTARY
Equities
Global equity markets in June 2026 were driven by a sharp shift in macroeconomic expectations, with the
S&P 500 Index declining 1.1% for the month as higher interest rates and geopolitical risks offset strong
earnings momentum. US 10-year Treasury yields rose above 4.5% at certain points during the month,
affecting duration-sensitive assets. Brent crude oil prices briefly surged above US$115 per barrel on fears of
disruption to the Strait of Hormuz before retreating towards the low US$70 range following the Iran peace
framework and reopening of shipping lanes. Meanwhile, the Japanese yen experienced heightened volatility
around the 160 level against the US dollar as the Bank of Japan raised interest rates by 25 basis points to
1.00%, the highest level since 1995.
Asian equity markets were divided between beneficiaries of AI-related tailwinds and those facing broader
macroeconomic challenges. Taiwan gained 3.1% in June 2026, supported by semiconductor capital
expenditure, stronger memory pricing and robust export data. Japan’s Nikkei Index advanced 5.6% for the
month on demand related to AI infrastructure. South Korea was broadly flat over the month but experienced
considerable volatility, as strength in memory-related sectors was later offset by a broader re-rating of the
semiconductor sector following weaker AI-related guidance from a major technology company and a more
hawkish shift in Fed expectations. Hong Kong declined 9.1% in June 2026, making it the weakest-performing
major Asian market as offshore China sentiment weakened amid capital outflows and soft domestic
economic conditions. India gained 2.3% for the month, reflecting relative insulation from semiconductor
cycle dynamics and continued support from domestic earnings growth.
ASEAN equity markets delivered mixed performance in June 2026, with Singapore emerging as the regional
outperformer and Indonesia the weakest market. Singapore rose 2.6% for the month, outperforming much of
Asia as investors viewed the market as a relative safe haven and increased allocations to the banking and
industrial sectors. Indonesia declined 7.9% in June 2026, affected by capital outflows, weaker commodity
sentiment and a pronounced risk-off move across regional cyclical sectors.

FUND MANAGER'S COMMENTARY
Sukuk
Markets navigated a volatile but ultimately constructive month in June 2026 as geopolitical tensions eased
materially. Early June was dominated by concerns surrounding the US-Iran conflict and potential
disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz. However, sentiment improved significantly after the US and Iran reached
a ceasefire framework and memorandum of understanding in mid-June, including steps towards
reopening shipping lanes.
US Treasuries experienced significant intra-month volatility during June 2026. The front end of the yield
curve remained pressured by stronger-than-expected economic data and persistent inflation concerns.
Meanwhile, longer-dated yields saw some reprieve, with 10-year US Treasury yields declining towards 4.40%
by the end of June, supported by geopolitical developments and evolving views on monetary policy under
the new Fed Chair, including the reaffirmation of the Fed’s commitment to maintaining price stability.
The USD sukuk market, as measured by the Bloomberg Emerging Markets (EM) Gulf Cooperation Council
(GCC) USD Sukuk Index, returned 0.37% for June 2026. Sentiment improved following the geopolitical de-
escalation, as evidenced by the recovery in primary market activity, with several high-yield and Additional
Tier 1 (AT1) issuances coming to market during the month.
Despite the conflict, Saudi Arabia’s headline Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) rose to 53.3 in June 2026, a
four-month high that remained comfortably within expansionary territory. The UAE PMI eased slightly to 50.8
but continued to signal expansion.
The sukuk portion of the portfolio maintained a weighted duration of 4.5 years as at the end of June 2026,
while the overall portfolio yield stood at 5.14%.

FUND MANAGER'S COMMENTARY
Outlook and Strategy
While the US-Iran ceasefire and reopening of the Strait of Hormuz have reduced immediate tail risks, the
situation remains fragile and susceptible to setbacks. Any renewed disruption to energy exports could
reignite volatility across asset classes.
US employment data for June 2026 surprised to the downside, with payroll growth of 57,000 compared to
market expectations of 113,000. As a result, further Fed interest rate hikes in the near term have become
significantly less likely based on market pricing. Nevertheless, investors continue to await more convincing
evidence of a sustained moderation in inflation following the ceasefire.
Overall, we believe the investment environment continues to favour a carry-oriented approach. While
geopolitical risks have moderated and credit fundamentals remain supportive, uncertainty surrounding
inflation and the future path of central bank policy remains elevated. As such, we continue to maintain a
neutral duration position.
Given the fragile nature of the ceasefire, we prefer to remain invested in high-quality USD-denominated
credit instruments within the short- to intermediate-maturity segment of the curve to capture attractive
carry opportunities. We continue to favour selected GCC quasi-sovereign issuers, as well as GCC banking
sector Tier 2 (T2) and AT1 securities for additional yield enhancement. Meanwhile, the average credit rating
of the sukuk portfolio remained investment grade at BBB+.
Equities
June’s market rotation supports maintaining equity exposure, with a preference for strong balance sheets,
earnings visibility and beneficiaries of AI-related growth trends within Asia. We continue to favour Taiwan,
South Korea and China, where semiconductor demand and policy support remain key earnings drivers,
while maintaining a constructive view on Singapore as a relative safe haven. India remains a useful source
of diversification through domestically driven growth opportunities. Hong Kong continues to present the
weakest regional signal, and we remain cautious on offshore China-related risks, retail property exposures
and sectors sensitive to capital outflows.
Taiwan remains a key beneficiary of the AI hardware cycle, supported by capital expenditure trends,
favourable memory pricing and resilient export data. As such, we continue to favour semiconductor and
advanced packaging segments. South Korea also merits an overweight allocation, although a selective
approach remains appropriate. China’s outlook continues to improve on stronger domestic activity and
policy support, leading us to favour selected exposures in energy, banking, domestic semiconductors and
clean-energy exporters. India should continue to benefit from domestic manufacturing and infrastructure
trends, although selectivity remains important in consumer-facing sectors where margin pressures may
persist.
The key risks heading into July 2026 include a renewed rise in US interest rates and any fresh disruptions to
shipping routes or energy markets. Key catalysts include sustained AI-related capital expenditure, further
stabilisation in semiconductor pricing and broader improvement in China’s domestic demand. Should Brent
crude prices remain around the low US$70 range and the US dollar stay relatively firm, exporters, banks and
infrastructure-related sectors are likely to remain supported. Our positioning therefore remains selectively
pro-risk, anchored in Taiwan, South Korea and China, complemented by other sources of durable earnings
growth.
