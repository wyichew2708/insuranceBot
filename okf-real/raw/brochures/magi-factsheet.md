FUND MANAGER'S COMMENTARY
Equity markets experienced a modest correction in June 2026 following the V-shaped recovery seen in April
2026. US equity markets declined between 1% and 3% during the month, with the technology-heavy NASDAQ
Index recording a larger decline. Asian equities also fell by approximately 2% in June 2026, primarily weighed
down by Hong Kong-listed Chinese stocks. Despite the pullback, equity markets performed strongly during
the first half of 2026, with Asian markets gaining more than 20% YTD and US markets rising around 10% YTD.
Fixed income markets faced a more challenging environment, delivering only marginal gains in 2026 as
persistent inflationary pressures, driven by higher commodity prices, remained a headwind. Against this
backdrop, the fund successfully captured the strong rally in Asian equities and generated returns in the mid-
teens during the first half of 2026.
While financial markets have delivered strong gains in 2026, volatility has remained elevated due to
geopolitical concerns. Tensions in the Middle East escalated after a military attack on Iran at the end of
March 2026, prompting retaliatory actions that disrupted regional stability and led to the closure of the Strait
of Hormuz, restricting shipping activity. The waterway is a critical component of the global energy supply
chain, handling approximately 20% of worldwide energy flows. The resulting supply disruption pushed oil
prices above USD100 per barrel and triggered a broad risk-off sentiment across financial markets. However,
strong corporate earnings from companies linked to AI during April and May 2026 supported a market
rebound, with major equity indices reaching record highs in May and June 2026. Overall market earnings
have been expanding by more than 20% year-on-year, while technology-related sectors have delivered
even stronger earnings growth exceeding 30% year-on-year.
Following three months of hostilities, there were signs of progress towards peace, culminating in the signing
of a memorandum of understanding (MOU) in June 2026 between the US and Iran for a 60-day ceasefire to
facilitate negotiations towards a broader peace agreement. However, at the time of writing, the conflict has
resumed. Nevertheless, we continue to believe that a ceasefire is likely to materialise. Our view is based on
the gradual depletion of US oil reserves. Although the US remains the world’s largest oil producer, domestic
consumption continues to exceed production. Current production stands at approximately 14 million barrels
per day, compared to consumption of 21 million barrels per day, resulting in a net deficit of 7–8 million barrels
per day. Assuming that 50% of the shortfall can be sourced externally, the remaining deficit would be
approximately 3–4 million barrels per day. Based on reserves of around 340 million barrels, we estimate that
the US has approximately 90–120 days of reserves remaining. As such, we believe there will be increasing
incentives to pursue a ceasefire that would allow the normal flow of oil and gas supplies to resume.
From an asset allocation perspective, we remain overweight equities, with an allocation of more than 60%,
which has benefited from the strong equity market rally. Our underweight position in fixed income has also
been supportive, as bond markets continue to face pressure from elevated interest rates driven by persistent
inflation. US headline inflation reached a 12-month high of 3.8% in June 2026, reducing the likelihood of near-
term interest rate cuts by the Fed. Consequently, interest rates have remained elevated, with yields on the
benchmark 10-year US Treasury hovering around 4.6%.
We continue to believe that corporate earnings will be the primary driver of equity market performance,
consistent with our 2026 investment theme of “Follow the earnings, follow the Fed”. We maintain our exposure
to Taiwan and South Korea and remain positive on the long-term prospects of the AI-related investment
theme. While AI-related stocks have undergone a sharp correction recently, partly reflecting speculative
activity by retail investors, we view this as a short-term adjustment. Equity markets in Taiwan and South
Korea have delivered strong gains throughout 2026 and are susceptible to profit-taking in the near term.
Nevertheless, we believe second-quarter 2026 earnings results, which are expected to be released in August
2026, could serve as a positive catalyst. Capex related to AI remains robust and should continue to benefit
Asia, given the region’s critical role within the global technology supply chain.
We remain overweight Singapore, where we continue to identify attractive opportunities within the small-
and mid-capitalisation segment. This area of the market has benefited from the Equity Market Development
Programme (EQDP) introduced by the MAS. The EQDP was launched in 2025 with an initial allocation of SGD5
billion, which was subsequently increased by an additional SGD1.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026. We are
also increasingly constructive on India, the Philippines and Thailand. In our view, the worst of the oil price
shock has likely passed, and as major net oil importers, these economies stand to benefit from moderating
energy prices, which could support a relief rally in their respective markets.
