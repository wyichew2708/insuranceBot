# The Ultimate Pre-Flight Checklist: 7 Questions Every Singaporean Should Ask Before Travelling

## The Ultimate Pre-Flight Checklist: 7 Questions Every Singaporean Should Ask Before Travelling

- Date: August 5, 2026

Planning a holiday today involves much more than packing your suitcase. Whether you’re heading to Bangkok for a long weekend, embarking on a self-drive adventure in Australia, or exploring the food scene in China, a smooth trip often comes down to the details many travellers overlook. From mobile connectivity and payment methods to transport planning and local travel requirements, a little preparation can go a long way. Before your next flight, run through these seven practical questions to help avoid unnecessary surprises after you land.

Your Pre-Flight Checklist at a Glance:

- Mobile Connectivity

- Payment Methods

- Essential Travel Apps

- Baggage Delays

- Travel Insurance

- Medical Emergencies

- Local Rules & Travel Tips

## 1. Have You Sorted Your Mobile Connectivity?

The first thing many travellers do after landing is reach for their phone. Whether you’re booking a ride, finding your hotel, translating a menu, or contacting family back home, internet access quickly becomes essential.

## Common Practices Worth Adopting

Before you leave Singapore:

- Download offline maps in case you temporarily lose internet access

- Save hotel confirmations, boarding passes, and key travel documents to your device

- Keep digital copies of your travel insurance documents

- Share your itinerary with a trusted family member or friend, especially if travelling solo

- Bring a power bank that complies with airline requirements. If you’re travelling to China, ensure your power bank carries a valid China Compulsory Certification (CCC)

## 2. How Will You Pay Overseas?

Once you’ve sorted your mobile connectivity, the next question is how you’ll pay for things when you arrive. Payment habits can vary across destinations, and assuming your credit card works everywhere can quickly turn into an inconvenience.

## China – Set Up WeChat Pay and Alipay

China is largely cashless, with WeChat and Alipay widely used for everything from restaurants and taxis to convenience stores and attractions. While major hotels and international brands may accept credit cards, many smaller businesses still rely primarily on QR code payments.

Before you travel, download and verify WeChat Pay or Alipay, and link an eligible international credit card. It’s also worth carrying a small amount of RMB as a backup, particularly when visiting local markets or smaller cities.

## Japan – Don’t Underestimate the Importance of Cash

Although Japan has become increasingly card-friendly, cash is still commonly used in many smaller restaurants, family-run shops, temples, shrines, rural attractions, and older establishments. Carrying sufficient Japanese Yen can save you from awkward situations, especially when travelling beyond major cities.

## South Korea – Cards Are Widely Accepted, but Keep a Backup Payment Option

South Korea is one of Asia’s most card-friendly destinations, with credit and debit cards accepted at most shops, restaurants, cafés, attractions, and hotels. That said, it’s still worth carrying a small amount of Korean Won, especially when visiting traditional markets, smaller eateries, or independent businesses.

## Europe – Card-Friendly Doesn’t Always Mean Cashless

Payment habits vary across Europe. While countries such as Sweden, Denmark, and Norway are largely cashless, some businesses in Germany, parts of Italy, and smaller towns may still prefer cash, particularly bakeries, family-run cafés, local markets, and public toilets. Carrying a small amount of local currency gives you added flexibility without relying entirely on cash.

## 3. Have You Downloaded These Essential Travel Apps Yet?

The right apps can make a noticeable difference to your travel experience. Whether you’re navigating unfamiliar streets, hailing a ride, translating conversations, or paying for everyday purchases, a few downloads can save time and minimise unnecessary hassle.

## Essential Apps by Destination

While many apps perform similar functions, the most popular options often differ by country. Using local apps can make it easier to get around, communicate, pay for purchases, or even order food like a local.

One destination worth paying extra attention to is mainland China . Popular Google services such as Google Maps, Gmail, Google Search, and YouTube are generally inaccessible without a VPN. If you rely on these services, download and test a reputable VPN before departure, as installing one after arrival can be challenging.

## Plan Your Arrival Before You Land

A smooth trip often starts long before you clear immigration. Spending a few minutes planning how you’ll get from the airport to your accommodation can help you avoid confusion, long queues, or expensive last-minute transport options.

Research the available airport trains, buses, taxis, and ride-hailing services in advance, especially if you’re arriving late at night. Not all airports operate around the clock. Before you fly, check:

- When the last train or bus departs

- Whether ride-hailing services are readily available

- If your hotel reception operates 24 hours

- Whether you’ll need to arrange a late check-in

Public Transport Cards

Many destinations use rechargeable transport cards that can also be used for convenience stores, vending machines, and selected attractions. Some can even be added to your Apple Wallet or Google Wallet before your trip.

- Japan: Suica, Pasmo, ICOCA

- South Korea: T-money

- Hong Kong: Octopus

- London: Oyster

## Check Immigration Requirements Before Departure

Many destinations now require travellers to complete digital arrival forms or travel declarations before they fly. Missing this step could result in processing delays at immigration or complications during your journey.

Before travelling, check whether your destination requires an online declaration or digital arrival card. Examples include:

- Thailand: Thailand Digital Arrival Card

- Japan: Visit Japan Web

- South Korea: Korea e-Arrival

- Australia: AustralianETA app

## 4. Could You Cope Without Your Luggage for a Day?

Even with careful planning, some things remain outside your control. Baggage delays are one of them, and they’re more common than many travellers realise. According to SITA’s 2026 Baggage IT Insights report found that 24 million bags were mishandled worldwide in 2025 , with around 70% of cases involving delayed baggage .

A delayed suitcase can quickly disrupt your plans, especially if it contains clothing, medication, toiletries, or other daily essentials. Having to replace these items overseas can also lead to unexpected expenses.

To minimise inconvenience, consider packing a few essentials in your carry-on luggage, including:

- A change of clothes

- Essential toiletries

- Medication

- Phone charger and charging cable

- Travel documents

- Valuables or electronics

## 5. Have You Sorted Your Travel Insurance?

Travel insurance is often one of the last things people purchase before a trip, but it can make a significant difference when plans don’t go as expected. From medical emergencies and flight disruptions to lost baggage and trip cancellations , unexpected situations can quickly become costly without the right protection.

A common misconception is that travel insurance isn’t necessary for short holidays, or that coverage provided by a credit card or airline is sufficient. In reality, these benefits may come with limitations or exclusions, making it important to understand exactly what’s covered before you travel.

## 6. Are You Prepared for Medical Emergencies?

Most trips go exactly as planned. But when they don’t, knowing where to get help can make all the difference. Medical expenses are often only one part of the picture. Depending on the situation, you may also need to extend your accommodation, rearrange flights, pay for additional transport, or have a loved one travel over to support you.

Before your trip, take a few minutes to familiarise yourself with the medical facilities near your accommodation. Knowing where to seek help and how far away it is can save valuable time in an emergency. Many destinations also have international clinics or hospitals that cater to foreign visitors and offer English-speaking services.

## 7. Do You Know the Local Rules Where You’re Going?

Every destination has its own customs, everyday habits, and travel quirks. Spending just a few minutes understanding them before you travel can help you blend in more easily, avoid unnecessary confusion, and make the most of your trip.

Here are a few examples worth knowing:

## China – Make the Most of Local Apps

Apps like Meituan are used for much more than food delivery. You can also order groceries, pharmacy items, daily necessities, and even flowers, often with surprisingly fast delivery.

Many Singaporeans also take the opportunity to shop on Taobao before or during their trip, arranging for purchases to be delivered directly to their hotel. If you plan to do the same, check with your accommodation beforehand to ensure they accept parcels on behalf of guests, and avoid ordering more than you can reasonably bring home.

## Japan – Follow Local Etiquette

Japanese culture places great emphasis on consideration for others. Keep conversations quiet on public transport, queue patiently, and dispose of your rubbish responsibly, as public bins can be surprisingly scarce. One exception? When it comes to noodles, slurping is perfectly acceptable and often seen as a sign that you’re enjoying your meal.

## Europe – Validate Your Train Tickets

In several European countries, purchasing a train ticket isn’t always enough. Some rail operators require passengers to validate their ticket before boarding, and failing to do so may result in a fine, even if you’ve already paid for your journey.

## Some Destinations Require Vaccinations

If you’re travelling to destinations such as Zimbabwe or Tanzania , you may need recommended or mandatory vaccinations , such as Yellow Fever , depending on your travel history and transit route. Always check the latest health and entry requirements before you fly.

## Travel Like a Local

Simple customs can differ from one destination to another. Whether it’s tipping practices or using local payment methods, understanding these small cultural differences can make your trip smoother and more enjoyable.

## Ready for Take-off?

Travel surprises aren’t always bad, but the avoidable ones usually are. Taking a little time to check the essentials before you leave can help you spend less time solving problems and more time making memories.

## Frequently Asked Questions

## Do I need a VPN when travelling to China?

If you rely on Google services such as Google Search, Gmail, Google Maps, or YouTube, yes . These services may not be accessible in mainland China, so it’s best to install and test a reputable VPN before you leave Singapore, as downloading one after arrival can be difficult.

## What should I pack in my carry-on?

Pack a change of clothes, essential toiletries, medication, travel documents, valuables, chargers, and any other items you’ll need during your first 24 hours. This helps minimise disruption if your checked baggage is delayed or arrives on a later flight.

## Do I need travel insurance for short trips?

Travel insurance is worth considering even for short trips. Medical emergencies, flight delays, baggage delays, and trip cancellations can happen regardless of how long you’re travelling, and the resulting costs can quickly add up.

## Should I exchange cash before travelling overseas?

Yes, it’s a good idea to carry some local currency, even if your destination is largely cashless. Small businesses, public transport, local markets, or unexpected situations may still require cash, so having a backup can be helpful.
